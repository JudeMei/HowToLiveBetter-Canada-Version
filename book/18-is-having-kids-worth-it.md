# 18. Is Having Kids Worth It?

> The decision to have children is personal, but the financial and time costs are measurable. This chapter outlines the benefits, costs, and supports available to Canadian parents.

---

### 1. Apply for the Canada Child Benefit (CCB) immediately upon birth
- Cost: Free. (Apply when registering the birth).
- Plain language: The CCB is a tax-free monthly payment made to eligible families to help with the cost of raising children under 18.
- Payoff: Can be up to $7,437 per year, per child under 6 (and up to $6,275 for children 6-17), depending on your family net income.
- Evidence: A (Federal Policy)
- Source: CRA. Canada Child Benefit.
- Note: You must file your tax return every year to get it, even if your income is zero. The amount decreases as your family income increases, but most Canadian families qualify for some amount.

### 2. Understand Maternity and Parental Leave (EI)
- Cost: You receive 55% or 33% of your earnings, depending on the option chosen, up to a maximum.
- Plain language: Under EI, you can take Standard parental leave (up to 40 weeks total between two parents, paid at 55% of earnings up to a max of $668/week) or Extended parental leave (up to 69 weeks total, paid at 33% up to $401/week). This is in addition to 15 weeks of Maternity leave for the birth mother.
- Payoff: Allows paid time off to care for a newborn.
- Evidence: A (Federal Policy)
- Source: ESDC. EI maternity and parental benefits.
- Note: Some employers offer "top-up" payments to bring your income closer to 100% during leave. Check your HR policies.

### 3. Open an RESP and get the Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG)
- Cost: Your contribution amount.
- Plain language: As noted in the financial chapter, the government matches 20% of your contributions to a child's Registered Education Savings Plan (RESP), up to $500 per year, plus extra bonds for low-income families.
- Payoff: Free money for their future education, growing tax-free.
- Evidence: A (Federal Policy)
- Note: Open this as soon as the child gets a SIN.

### 4. Look into the Canada-Wide Early Learning and Child Care system ($10/day daycare)
- Cost: Heavily subsidized daycare fees.
- Plain language: The federal and provincial governments are rolling out a system aiming to bring licensed childcare costs down to an average of $10/day by 2026. Fees have already been reduced by at least 50% in most participating provinces.
- Payoff: Saves thousands of dollars a year compared to previous childcare costs.
- Evidence: A (Federal/Provincial Policy)
- Source: Government of Canada. Toward $10-a-day.
- Note: Waitlists for licensed spots are notoriously long. Get on waitlists while you are pregnant.

### 5. The Time Cost: Expect a massive shift in free time
- Cost: Several hours per day.
- Plain language: Studies on time use show that parents of young children experience a significant decrease in leisure time and an increase in unpaid work (childcare, housework).
- Payoff: Emotional fulfillment, but a temporary reduction in personal autonomy.
- Evidence: B
- Source: Statistics Canada. Time Use surveys.
- Note: The intense time requirement decreases as children enter school, but shifts toward logistical coordination (extracurriculars).

### 6. The Financial Cost: Budget ~$10,000 to $15,000 per year per child
- Cost: Significant financial commitment.
- Plain language: Various estimates put the cost of raising a child to age 18 in Canada between $250,000 and $350,000, factoring in housing, food, clothing, and childcare (though the new childcare subsidies reduce this).
- Payoff: N/A.
- Evidence: B
- Source: MoneySense; various economic estimates.
- Note: Much of this cost is incremental housing (needing an extra bedroom). You can save massively on clothes and gear by buying second-hand, as infants outgrow items in months.
