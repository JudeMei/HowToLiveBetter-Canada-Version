# 23. Which Skills Pay Off

> Education is an investment of time and money. This chapter covers the Canadian realities of which skills actually generate a return.

---

### 1. Check the National Occupational Classification (NOC) and Job Bank for demand
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Before spending 4 years and $40,000 on a degree, look up the actual job prospects. The Government of Canada Job Bank publishes detailed 3-year outlooks for every profession in every region, including average wages.
- Payoff: Prevents training for an oversaturated field.
- Evidence: A (Labour Market Data)
- Source: Government of Canada Job Bank. Trend Analysis.
- Note: If the outlook in your province is "Limited," you will likely struggle to find work in that exact field upon graduation.

### 2. Consider the Skilled Trades (Apprenticeships)
- Cost: You get paid while you learn.
- Plain language: University leaves many graduates with debt and no clear career path. The skilled trades (electrician, plumber, HVAC, heavy duty mechanic) involve an apprenticeship where you work and earn a wage while learning, interspersed with short periods of trade school (often heavily subsidized).
- Payoff: High earning potential, zero student debt, and massive demand as older tradespeople retire.
- Evidence: A (Labour Market Data)
- Source: Statistics Canada. Registered apprenticeship training programs.
- Note: Look into the Red Seal Program. A Red Seal endorsement means your trade qualification is recognized across Canada, allowing you to move to wherever the work pays best without re-certifying.

### 3. Apply for Government Apprenticeship Grants
- Cost: Free to apply.
- Plain language: The federal government offers the Apprenticeship Incentive Grant ($1,000 per level, up to $2,000) and the Apprenticeship Completion Grant ($2,000 when you get your journeyperson certification).
- Payoff: Up to $4,000 in free money for completing your trades training.
- Evidence: A
- Source: ESDC. Apprenticeship Grants.
- Note: There are also specific grants for women in underrepresented trades.

### 4. Know the ROI of your University Degree
- Cost: Zero to research.
- Plain language: Not all degrees are financially equal. Statistics Canada data consistently shows that graduates in STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Math) and Business/Healthcare have significantly higher lifetime earnings than graduates in Humanities and Fine Arts.
- Payoff: Informed financial decision-making. If you study a low-ROI field, you must accept that the payoff is personal/intellectual, not financial, and borrow accordingly.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Statistics Canada. Earnings of postsecondary graduates by detailed field of study.
- Note: If you want to study humanities, aim to pair it with hard skills (e.g., a minor in computer science, statistics, or accounting) to increase employability.

### 5. Look into Second Career / Better Jobs programs if you are laid off
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: If you lose your job and your skills are outdated, provinces offer retraining programs (e.g., Better Jobs Ontario). They can pay up to $28,000 for tuition, books, and basic living allowances while you go back to school for a high-demand field.
- Payoff: Free retraining to get back into the workforce.
- Evidence: A (Provincial Policy)
- Source: Provincial Ministry of Labour/Training websites.
- Note: The application process is rigorous; you must prove that the new career has good local job prospects and that you have researched the training program.

### 6. Newcomers: Get your credentials assessed immediately
- Cost: ~$200–$300 for an ECA (Educational Credential Assessment).
- Plain language: If you were a nurse, engineer, or accountant in your home country, you cannot practice in Canada until the relevant provincial regulatory body recognizes your credentials. Start this process *before* you arrive, or immediately after.
- Payoff: Avoids getting stuck in "survival jobs" for years.
- Evidence: A (Immigration Policy)
- Source: IRCC. Educational credential assessment.
- Note: Use a designated organization like WES (World Education Services) or ICAS. Regulatory bodies for regulated professions (like nursing or engineering) have specific pathways you must follow.
