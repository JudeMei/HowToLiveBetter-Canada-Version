# 17. Aging Parents

> Managing the health, finances, and living situation of aging parents is a complex logistical challenge. Prepare before the crisis hits.

---

### 1. Ensure your parents have Powers of Attorney (POA) in place while they are still capable
- Cost: ~$200–$500 to have a lawyer draft them (can be done alongside a Will).
- Plain language: A Will only takes effect when someone dies. If your parent has a stroke or develops dementia and loses capacity, a Will is useless. You need a POA for Property (to access their bank accounts to pay their bills) and a POA for Personal Care (to make medical decisions).
- Payoff: If they lose capacity and don't have a POA, you cannot legally manage their affairs without applying to the court for guardianship — a process that costs thousands of dollars and takes months.
- Evidence: A (Estate Law)
- Source: Provincial Ministries of the Attorney General.
- Note: The parent must be mentally capable at the time they sign the POA. Do it now.

### 2. Check their eligibility for the Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS)
- Cost: Free to apply.
- Plain language: The GIS is a monthly non-taxable benefit for low-income seniors who are receiving Old Age Security (OAS). If your parents have very little income other than CPP and OAS, they likely qualify.
- Payoff: Up to ~$1,000 extra per month (tax-free) depending on income and marital status.
- Evidence: A (Federal Policy)
- Source: Employment and Social Development Canada. GIS.
- Note: You must file a tax return every year to keep receiving it. If they haven't filed taxes in years, they are missing out on this money.

### 3. Have the "Driving" conversation before an accident happens
- Cost: Emotional difficulty.
- Plain language: Driving is a core symbol of independence, but cognitive and physical decline make it dangerous. Look for unexplained dents on the car, getting lost on familiar routes, or delayed reaction times.
- Payoff: Prevents fatal car accidents.
- Evidence: C
- Note: You can request that their family doctor evaluate their fitness to drive. Doctors are legally required (in most provinces) to report medically unfit drivers to the Ministry of Transportation, which takes the burden of "taking away the keys" off you.

### 4. Remove tripping hazards in their home
- Cost: Zero to ~$50 for non-slip mats.
- Plain language: Falls are the leading cause of injury-related hospitalizations among Canadian seniors. A hip fracture often triggers a permanent move to a long-term care facility and a significant decline in life expectancy.
- Payoff: An RCT (Clemson L et al., 2008) found that environmental modifications for seniors at risk of falling reduced falls by 39%.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Clemson L et al. (2008). Environmental interventions for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database Syst Rev.
- Note: Remove throw rugs (or tape them down securely). Install grab bars in the bathroom. Ensure stairwells are well-lit. Make sure they have a clear path to the bathroom at night.

### 5. Access provincial Home Care services before considering a nursing home
- Cost: Free (covered by provincial health systems).
- Plain language: Every province has a publicly funded home care system (e.g., Home and Community Care Support Services in Ontario, CLSC in Quebec). They can send personal support workers (PSWs) to help with bathing, dressing, and nursing care at home.
- Payoff: Delays or prevents the need for expensive and disruptive Long-Term Care.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Provincial Ministries of Health.
- Note: You do not need a doctor's referral to access home care assessments; you or your parent can call the local agency directly to request an evaluation.

### 6. Protect them from scams
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Seniors are disproportionately targeted by scams (the "Grandparent Scam" where someone calls claiming a grandchild is in jail and needs bail money in iTunes cards or crypto; CRA scams; romance scams).
- Payoff: Prevents the loss of their entire life savings.
- Evidence: B
- Source: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre.
- Note: Establish a "family password." Tell them if someone ever calls claiming you are in trouble and asking for money, they must ask for the password. If the caller doesn't know it, it's a scam.
