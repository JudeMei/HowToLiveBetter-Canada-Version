# 9. Legal Lines Ordinary People Cross

> The Criminal Code of Canada contains offences that ordinary people commit because they don't realize how strict the law is. This chapter covers the boundaries.

---

### 1. Do not carry anything for self-defence (mace, pepper spray, tasers, batons)
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Carrying any weapon for the purpose of self-defence against humans is illegal in Canada. "Bear spray" is legal to carry in the woods for bears; carrying it in downtown Toronto for protection is a crime (Carrying a Concealed Weapon / Possession of a Weapon for a Dangerous Purpose).
- Payoff: Avoids a criminal record and up to 10 years in prison.
- Evidence: A (Criminal Code)
- Source: Criminal Code of Canada, ss. 88, 90.
- Note: Even items that aren't inherently weapons (like a baseball bat or a heavy flashlight) become illegal weapons the moment you carry them *with the intent* to use them for self-defence.

### 2. Do not share or forward intimate images without consent
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: "Revenge porn" is a crime. Forwarding a nude photo of someone else that was sent to you, without the subject's explicit consent, is a criminal offence.
- Payoff: Avoids a criminal record, up to 5 years in prison, and sex offender registration.
- Evidence: A (Criminal Code)
- Source: Criminal Code of Canada, s. 162.1.
- Note: It doesn't matter if the person originally sent the photo to you willingly. The consent must cover *distributing* the image.

### 3. Do not record a conversation unless you are a part of it (One-Party Consent)
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Canada is a "one-party consent" state. You can secretly record a conversation *as long as you are one of the people in the conversation*. You CANNOT leave a recorder in a room to record two other people talking without their knowledge.
- Payoff: Avoids charges for interception of private communications (wiretapping).
- Evidence: A (Criminal Code)
- Source: Criminal Code of Canada, s. 184.
- Note: While legal criminally, secretly recording your boss or coworkers can be grounds for termination for cause (breach of trust) in employment law.

### 4. Do not lie on a mortgage or loan application
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Inflating your income or lying about your down payment source to get a mortgage is fraud. Even if you make all the payments on time, the bank can call the loan immediately if they find out, and you can be criminally charged.
- Payoff: Avoids fraud charges and foreclosure.
- Evidence: A (Criminal Code)
- Source: Criminal Code of Canada, s. 362 (False pretences).
- Note: Mortgage brokers who tell you to "fudge the numbers" are involving you in mortgage fraud.

### 5. Do not fly a drone (over 250g) without a certificate
- Cost: $10 for the exam.
- Plain language: Flying a drone that weighs more than 250g (like a DJI Mavic) requires passing an online Transport Canada exam and registering the drone. Flying near airports or over crowds is strictly prohibited.
- Payoff: Avoids fines of up to $3,000 for individuals ($15,000 for corporations).
- Evidence: A (Canadian Aviation Regulations)
- Source: Transport Canada. Drone safety.
- Note: Even for micro-drones (under 250g), you cannot fly them in a way that poses a risk to aviation safety or people.

### 6. Defamation: Be careful what you post online (Libel)
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: You can be sued for posting a negative Google review or a Facebook post if it contains false factual statements that harm a business's or person's reputation.
- Payoff: Avoids civil lawsuits and damages.
- Evidence: A (Tort Law)
- Note: "Truth" is a complete defence to defamation. "Fair comment" (stating an opinion based on true facts) is also a defence. If you write a review, stick strictly to the facts you can prove, and frame subjective thoughts as your opinion ("In my opinion, the service was rude" vs. "The owner steals from customers").

### 7. Do not let someone use your bank account to transfer money
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: If an online "job" or a person you met on a dating app asks you to receive a transfer, keep a cut, and send the rest elsewhere (via crypto or Western Union), you are acting as a money mule. This is money laundering.
- Payoff: Avoids criminal charges for money laundering and having your bank accounts permanently closed.
- Evidence: A (Criminal Code)
- Source: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre; Criminal Code of Canada, s. 462.31.
- Note: The bank will eventually reverse the fraudulent deposit, you will owe the bank the full amount, and you may face criminal investigation. Ignorance is rarely an accepted defence if you were willfully blind.
