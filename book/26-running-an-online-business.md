# 26. Running an Online Business

> Operating a website or platform in Canada triggers specific legal and tax obligations. This chapter covers the essentials to keep you out of trouble.

---

### 1. Register for GST/HST/QST when selling digital products
- Cost: Zero to register.
- Plain language: If your worldwide revenue exceeds $30,000 CAD over four consecutive calendar quarters, you must register for and collect GST/HST on sales made to Canadian residents. (Quebec has its own rules for QST).
- Payoff: Avoids crippling back-taxes and penalties during a CRA audit.
- Evidence: A (Excise Tax Act)
- Source: CRA. E-commerce: Sales of digital products or services.
- Note: If you sell to customers in the US or Europe, you generally do not charge them Canadian GST/HST, but those sales still count toward your $30,000 threshold, and you may be liable for foreign taxes (like EU VAT) depending on their rules.

### 2. Comply with CASL (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation)
- Cost: Minor development/setup time.
- Plain language: You cannot email marketing newsletters to Canadians without their consent (express or implied). You must clearly identify your business and include an easy unsubscribe mechanism.
- Payoff: Avoids fines that can reach into the millions for corporations.
- Evidence: A (Federal Law)
- Source: CRTC. CASL.
- Note: Implied consent exists if they bought something from you in the last 24 months, but express consent (a checked box, not pre-checked) is always safer.

### 3. Comply with PIPEDA (Privacy Law)
- Cost: Minor legal/setup time.
- Plain language: The Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) applies to commercial activities in Canada. You must have a clear Privacy Policy detailing what data you collect, why you collect it, and who you share it with.
- Payoff: Legal compliance and customer trust.
- Evidence: A (Federal Law)
- Source: Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.
- Note: You must obtain meaningful consent to collect data, and you must protect it with adequate security safeguards. If you suffer a data breach involving sensitive information, you are legally required to report it to the Privacy Commissioner and the affected individuals.

### 4. Understand that you are liable for the content you host (to a degree)
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: If you run a platform that hosts user-generated content (like a forum or social network), you can be held liable for defamation or copyright infringement (Notice and Notice regime) if you fail to act when notified.
- Payoff: Avoids civil lawsuits.
- Evidence: A (Copyright Modernization Act, Defamation Law)
- Note: Canada does not have an exact equivalent to the US "Section 230" blanket immunity. If someone complains that a user posted defamatory content on your site, you may need to remove it to protect yourself from liability. Consult a lawyer.

### 5. Secure your domain name (.ca requirements)
- Cost: ~$15/year.
- Plain language: To register a .ca domain, you must meet the Canadian Presence Requirements (e.g., be a Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or a corporation registered in Canada).
- Payoff: Ensures your domain cannot be stripped from you by CIRA.
- Evidence: A (CIRA Policy)
- Source: Canadian Internet Registration Authority (CIRA).
- Note: Keep your domain registration contact info up to date. Losing your domain because you missed a renewal email can destroy your business.

### 6. Accessibility compliance (AODA and others)
- Cost: Variable (web development).
- Plain language: If you operate in Ontario, the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) requires public-facing websites of organizations with 50+ employees to meet WCAG 2.0 Level AA accessibility standards. Other provinces and the federal government are rolling out similar legislation.
- Payoff: Avoids fines (up to $100,000 for corporations in Ontario) and makes your site usable by millions of Canadians with disabilities.
- Evidence: A (Provincial Law)
- Source: Ontario.ca. Accessibility rules for businesses and non-profits.
- Note: Key requirements include sufficient colour contrast, keyboard navigability, and alt text for images.
