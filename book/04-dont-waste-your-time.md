# 4. Don't Waste Your Time

> Time is the only resource you can never earn back. This chapter covers the cognitive biases that make us waste time and practical strategies to reclaim it.

---

### 1. Ignore "sunk costs" — don't throw good time after bad
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Just because you've already spent two years on a degree you hate, or 40 minutes watching a terrible movie, doesn't mean you should keep going. The time is gone either way. Cut your losses.
- Payoff: Prevents years of wasted effort. The "sunk cost fallacy" is a cognitive bias where people continue an endeavor once an investment in money, effort, or time has been made, despite evidence that it's no longer the best choice.
- Evidence: B
- Source: Arkes HR, Blumer C. (1985). The psychology of sunk cost. Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes. 35(1):124-140.
- Note: Ask yourself: "If I hadn't already invested [X time/money] into this, would I start doing it today?" If no, stop.

### 2. Implement "Implementation Intentions" to beat procrastination
- Cost: Takes 2 minutes to write down.
- Plain language: Don't just say "I'll exercise more." Write down: "When I finish work at 5:00 PM on Tuesday, I will put on my shoes and run for 20 minutes."
- Payoff: Meta-analyses show a medium-to-large effect size (d = 0.65) for implementation intentions in achieving goals compared to just having a goal. It links the desired behavior to a specific cue, bypassing the need for willpower in the moment.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Gollwitzer PM, Sheeran P. (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. Advances in Experimental Social Psychology. 38:69-119.
- Note: The formula is: "If [situation Y] arises, then I will perform [response Z]."

### 3. Use "Commitment Devices" to lock yourself into good choices
- Cost: Variable (e.g., pre-paying for a class).
- Plain language: Make the bad choice harder or more expensive. Leave your credit cards at home when you go to the mall. Delete social media apps off your phone and only check them on a computer.
- Payoff: Behavioral economics shows that people who use commitment devices are significantly more likely to achieve long-term goals (saving money, quitting smoking, exercising) because they anticipate their future lack of willpower.
- Evidence: B
- Source: Bryan G et al. (2010). Commitment Devices. Annual Review of Economics. 2:671-698.
- Note: A commitment device must have a cost (financial, reputational, or hassle) for failing to follow through.

### 4. Delete doomscrolling apps (or use app limits)
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: Mindless scrolling on social media consumes hours a day for the average adult, with no positive return and a negative impact on mental health.
- Payoff: The average Canadian spends ~2 hours a day on social media. Reducing this time frees up hundreds of hours a year. Observational studies link heavy social media use to increased rates of anxiety and depression.
- Evidence: B
- Source: Hunt MG et al. (2018). No More FOMO: Limiting Social Media Decreases Loneliness and Depression. Journal of Social and Clinical Psychology. 37(10):751-768.
- Note: If you must use them, set a hard 15-minute daily limit using your phone's built-in Screen Time / Digital Wellbeing features.

### 5. Calculate your real hourly wage (after taxes and commuting)
- Cost: 10 minutes of math.
- Plain language: If you make $30/hour, you don't actually take home $30. After income tax, CPP, EI, and the unpaid time and cost of commuting, your real wage might be $18/hour.
- Payoff: Changes how you value your time and purchases. If a gadget costs $180, ask: "Is this worth 10 hours of my actual life?"
- Evidence: C
- Source: Based on the concept of "Real Hourly Wage" from Vicki Robin and Joe Dominguez, *Your Money or Your Life*.
- Note: To calculate: Take your weekly take-home pay (after all deductions). Divide by (hours worked + hours spent commuting).

### 6. Decline meetings without a clear agenda
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Meetings without agendas are usually a waste of time. Ask for the agenda or decline.
- Payoff: Saves hours of unproductive time weekly. Research on workplace productivity consistently identifies poorly run meetings as a primary source of wasted time.
- Evidence: C
- Source: Rogelberg SG et al. (2006). "Not another meeting!" Are meeting time demands related to employee well-being? J Appl Psychol. 91(1):83-96.
- Note: If you must attend, ask if you can just attend the portion relevant to you.
