# 30. School-Age Children

> This chapter covers the critical health, safety, and psychological interventions for children from kindergarten through high school.

---

### 1. Enforce 2 hours of outdoor time daily to prevent severe myopia
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: Rates of nearsightedness (myopia) are skyrocketing. The strongest preventative measure is time spent outdoors in natural light. Looking at screens does not *cause* myopia; lack of sunlight exposure does.
- Payoff: Reduces the risk of severe myopia, which can lead to retinal detachment and blindness later in life.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Xiong S et al. (2017). Time spent in outdoor activities in relation to myopia prevention and control: a meta-analysis and systematic review. Acta Ophthalmol. 95(6):551-566.
- Note: The goal is 2 hours of daylight exposure.

### 2. Do not delay medical treatment for exams or school commitments
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: If your child is showing signs of a severe illness (e.g., extreme lethargy, unexplained weight loss, persistent severe headaches), do not say "We'll go to the doctor after finals."
- Payoff: Prevents tragic outcomes from delayed diagnosis of conditions like Type 1 Diabetes, leukemia, or brain tumors.
- Evidence: C (Pediatric consensus)
- Note: Schools have mechanisms to defer exams for medical emergencies. Health always comes first.

### 3. Take bullying seriously immediately
- Cost: Time and advocacy.
- Plain language: Chronic bullying physically changes a child's brain, leading to long-term anxiety, depression, and increased risk of suicide. It is not a "normal rite of passage."
- Payoff: Protects the child's psychological and physical safety.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Moore SE et al. (2017). Consequences of bullying victimization in childhood and adolescence: A systematic review and meta-analysis. World J Psychiatry. 7(1):60-76.
- Note: Escalate to the school principal. If the school refuses to act and the bullying involves physical assault, threats, or explicit images, involve the police. If necessary, change schools.

### 4. Have their eyes and teeth checked regularly
- Cost: Eye exams are free for children under 18 in most provinces. Dental care is covered for low-income families (CDCP, provincial programs).
- Plain language: A child who cannot see the board or who is in chronic pain from a cavity will act out or fail in school. Often, they don't know their vision is bad because they assume everyone sees the world that way.
- Payoff: Removes physical barriers to learning.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Canadian Association of Optometrists.
- Note: Children should have a comprehensive eye exam at age 3, and annually thereafter.

### 5. Protect their sleep (9-11 hours)
- Cost: Enforcing boundaries.
- Plain language: Sleep deprivation in children mimics the symptoms of ADHD (hyperactivity, inability to focus, emotional volatility).
- Payoff: Improves emotional regulation, academic performance, and physical growth.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Canadian Paediatric Society.
- Note: Remove all screens (phones, tablets, TVs) from the bedroom at least 1 hour before sleep. The blue light and the notifications destroy sleep architecture.

### 6. Discuss online safety and explicit content before they encounter it
- Cost: An awkward conversation.
- Plain language: By age 11, most children have been exposed to extreme pornography or violent content online, usually accidentally or shown by a peer. If you don't talk to them about it, their understanding of sex and relationships will be shaped by the internet.
- Payoff: Gives them a framework to process disturbing content and ensures they come to you if they are threatened online (e.g., sextortion).
- Evidence: B
- Source: MediaSmarts (Canada's Centre for Digital and Media Literacy).
- Note: The conversation is not a one-time lecture. Keep the lines of communication open so they aren't afraid of being punished if they encounter something bad.
