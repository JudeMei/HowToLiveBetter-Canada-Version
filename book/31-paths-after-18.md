# 31. Paths After 18

> The assumption that every 18-year-old must immediately go to university is a recent and expensive phenomenon. This chapter covers the viable, financially sound alternatives in Canada.

---

### 1. The Canadian Armed Forces (CAF)
- Cost: You get paid.
- Plain language: Joining the military (Regular Force or Reserves) provides immediate employment, a salary, and comprehensive benefits. You can join directly out of high school, or the CAF will pay for your university/college education (Paid Education programs like ROTP) in exchange for mandatory service years.
- Payoff: Zero student debt, guaranteed employment, and early retirement options.
- Evidence: A (Federal Policy)
- Source: Government of Canada. Canadian Armed Forces careers.
- Note: It is a massive commitment. You must meet physical and medical standards, and you can be deployed anywhere in the world. You cannot simply quit on a whim.

### 2. Skilled Trades Apprenticeships
- Cost: You earn a wage while learning.
- Plain language: As detailed in Chapter 23, the skilled trades offer a direct path to high-income careers without the debt of a university degree.
- Payoff: High lifetime earnings, strong job security.
- Evidence: A (Labour Market Data)
- Note: Reach out to local union halls or provincial apprenticeship authorities to find employers willing to take on a first-year apprentice.

### 3. College (Diplomas and Certificates)
- Cost: $3,000–$6,000/year (significantly cheaper than university).
- Plain language: Canadian colleges (e.g., Humber, SAIT, BCIT) focus on applied, career-ready skills (e.g., dental hygiene, IT networking, paramedicine).
- Payoff: Faster entry into the workforce with highly employable skills.
- Evidence: A (Labour Market Data)
- Note: Many college programs have higher post-graduation employment rates than general arts or science university degrees.

### 4. Working Holiday Visas (International Experience Canada)
- Cost: Travel and visa costs; you earn money abroad.
- Plain language: If you are a Canadian citizen between 18 and 30 (or 35 for some countries), you can get a Working Holiday Visa for countries like Australia, the UK, Japan, and New Zealand. It allows you to travel and work legally to fund your trip.
- Payoff: Global experience and personal growth without massive debt.
- Evidence: A (International Treaties)
- Source: IRCC. International Experience Canada.
- Note: A gap year spent working abroad can provide clarity on what you actually want to study before committing $40,000 to a degree.

### 5. Start a small business or freelance
- Cost: Variable (sweat equity).
- Plain language: The barrier to entry for digital businesses (web design, content creation) or local service businesses (landscaping, cleaning) is incredibly low.
- Payoff: Teaches you more about business, taxes, and self-reliance in one year than a 4-year business degree.
- Evidence: C
- Note: If you fail, you are 19 with no dependents. The risk is minimal.

### 6. University (If the ROI makes sense)
- Cost: $6,000–$15,000+/year for tuition, plus living expenses.
- Plain language: University is the correct path if your desired career *legally or practically requires it* (medicine, law, engineering, nursing, accounting).
- Payoff: High lifetime earnings for specific professional degrees.
- Evidence: A (Statistics Canada)
- Note: Do not go to university just because "it's what you do next." If you don't know what you want to do, work for a year first. Apply for OSAP/provincial student loans—they include non-repayable grants based on family income.
