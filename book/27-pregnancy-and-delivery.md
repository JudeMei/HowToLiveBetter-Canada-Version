# 27. Pregnancy & Delivery

> Pregnancy in Canada is heavily medicalized but highly safe. This chapter covers what to expect from the Canadian healthcare system and how to advocate for yourself.

---

### 1. Take Folic Acid before you get pregnant
- Cost: ~$10 for a bottle of supplements.
- Plain language: Taking a daily supplement containing 0.4 mg of folic acid for at least 2 to 3 months *before* conception and throughout the first trimester reduces the risk of neural tube defects (like spina bifida) by up to 70%.
- Payoff: Prevents severe congenital disabilities.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Society of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists of Canada (SOGC).
- Note: If you have a higher risk (e.g., family history, diabetes, epilepsy), your doctor may recommend a much higher dose (up to 4.0 mg or 5.0 mg daily).

### 2. Understand the timeline of Prenatal Care
- Cost: Free under provincial health care.
- Plain language: You will see your maternity care provider (family doctor, midwife, or obstetrician) every 4 weeks until 28 weeks, every 2 weeks until 36 weeks, and every week until delivery.
- Payoff: Regular monitoring catches complications (like preeclampsia or gestational diabetes) before they become life-threatening.
- Evidence: A (Standard of Care)
- Source: SOGC. Routine prenatal care.
- Note: Midwifery care is fully funded in most provinces (e.g., Ontario, BC, Alberta) and offers longer appointments and more personalized care, but spots fill up incredibly fast. Apply the moment you get a positive pregnancy test.

### 3. Get the routine prenatal screening (Ultrasounds and Blood Tests)
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: You will be offered screening for chromosomal abnormalities (e.g., Down syndrome) in the first trimester (eFTS or NIPT), an anatomy ultrasound around 18-22 weeks, and a glucose challenge test for gestational diabetes around 24-28 weeks.
- Payoff: Early detection allows for preparation or intervention.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Provincial prenatal screening programs (e.g., BORN Ontario).
- Note: NIPT (Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing) is a highly accurate blood test. It is free if you are considered high-risk in most provinces; otherwise, you can pay out of pocket (~$300-$500).

### 4. Know the signs that mean "Go to the Hospital immediately"
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Go to the ER or Labour & Delivery triage immediately if you experience: heavy vaginal bleeding, sudden severe abdominal pain, a sudden decrease in the baby's movements, your water breaks (especially if before 37 weeks or the fluid is green/brown), or signs of preeclampsia (severe headache, vision changes, sudden severe swelling).
- Payoff: Prevents stillbirth or maternal mortality.
- Evidence: A
- Source: SOGC.
- Note: Do not wait for your next appointment. The hospital is equipped to monitor the baby's heart rate continuously.

### 5. Understand your pain management options during delivery
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: In Canadian hospitals, you have access to nitrous oxide (laughing gas), IV pain medications (like fentanyl or morphine), and epidurals.
- Payoff: Manages the extreme pain of labor.
- Evidence: A
- Source: SOGC. Pain relief in labour.
- Note: An epidural is the most effective form of pain relief and is administered by an anesthesiologist. It does not increase the risk of needing a C-section, though it may slightly prolong the pushing phase.

### 6. Do not consume alcohol during pregnancy
- Cost: Saves money.
- Plain language: There is no known safe amount, safe time, or safe type of alcohol during pregnancy. It crosses the placenta and can cause Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD), leading to lifelong physical, behavioural, and cognitive issues.
- Payoff: Prevents irreversible brain damage to the fetus.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Public Health Agency of Canada.
- Note: If you drank before you knew you were pregnant, stop immediately. The risk decreases the sooner you stop.

### 7. File your EI Maternity/Parental benefits application right away
- Cost: Free to apply.
- Plain language: You can start your EI maternity benefits up to 12 weeks before your due date, or the week you give birth. Apply as soon as you stop working.
- Payoff: Ensures your income is replaced without a massive delay.
- Evidence: A (Administrative)
- Source: Service Canada.
- Note: Your employer must issue a Record of Employment (ROE) directly to Service Canada. Ensure they do this promptly.
