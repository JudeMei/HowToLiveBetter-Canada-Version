# 20. Caring for a Newborn

> The first few months are exhausting. This chapter covers the critical safety protocols and logistical tasks for new parents in Canada.

---

### 1. Always put the baby to sleep on their back, in an empty crib
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: The "Back to Sleep" campaign reduced Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) by over 50%. A safe sleep space is: a firm mattress, a fitted sheet, and nothing else. No bumpers, no blankets, no stuffed animals, no pillows.
- Payoff: Drastically reduces the risk of SIDS and accidental suffocation.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Public Health Agency of Canada. Joint Statement on Safe Sleep.
- Note: Co-sleeping (bed-sharing) increases the risk of SIDS, especially if the parents smoke, have consumed alcohol, or if the baby is under 4 months old. Room-sharing (baby in their own crib in your room) is recommended for the first 6 months.

### 2. Follow the routine immunization schedule
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: Childhood vaccines protect against diseases that used to kill thousands of infants (polio, pertussis, measles, Haemophilus influenzae type b).
- Payoff: Almost completely eliminates the risk of these infectious diseases.
- Evidence: A
- Source: National Advisory Committee on Immunization (NACI).
- Note: The schedule starts at 2 months of age. Do not delay or space out vaccines — this leaves the infant unprotected during their most vulnerable months.

### 3. Give breastfed babies Vitamin D drops daily
- Cost: ~$15 for a bottle of drops.
- Plain language: Breast milk is excellent, but it lacks sufficient Vitamin D (especially in Canada, where sun exposure is low). The Canadian Paediatric Society recommends 400 IU of Vitamin D daily for all breastfed infants.
- Payoff: Prevents rickets (a bone-softening disease).
- Evidence: A
- Source: Canadian Paediatric Society. Vitamin D guidelines.
- Note: Formula-fed babies do not need drops if they are drinking enough formula, as it is already fortified with Vitamin D.

### 4. Know the signs of neonatal jaundice that require a doctor
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Many newborns get mild jaundice (yellowing of the skin) which resolves on its own. However, severe jaundice can cause brain damage (kernicterus). If the yellowing spreads below the belly button, the baby is lethargic, or isn't feeding well, go to a doctor or ER immediately.
- Payoff: Prevents permanent neurological damage.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Canadian Paediatric Society. Guidelines for detection, management and prevention of hyperbilirubinemia.
- Note: Treatment (phototherapy) is simple, safe, and highly effective if caught early.

### 5. Never shake a baby (Shaken Baby Syndrome)
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Shaking a baby out of frustration causes the brain to bounce inside the skull, leading to bleeding, brain damage, blindness, or death.
- Payoff: Prevents catastrophic injury.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Public Health Agency of Canada.
- Note: If the baby won't stop crying and you feel yourself losing control, put the baby in a safe place (their crib), close the door, and walk away for 5-10 minutes to calm down. The crying won't hurt them; shaking will.

### 6. Do not give infants under 1 year old honey
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Honey can contain spores of Clostridium botulinum. Adult digestive systems can handle it, but an infant's cannot. It causes infant botulism, a rare but life-threatening paralysis.
- Payoff: Prevents botulism.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Health Canada. Infant Botulism.
- Note: This includes pasteurized honey. Wait until their first birthday.

### 7. Register the birth and apply for the SIN and CCB immediately
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: Most provinces have an integrated "Newborn Registration Service" online. You can register the birth, apply for the birth certificate, apply for a Social Insurance Number (SIN), and apply for the Canada Child Benefit (CCB) all in one form.
- Payoff: Gets you the CCB payments faster and secures the baby's legal identity.
- Evidence: A (Administrative)
- Source: Provincial newborn registration portals (e.g., ServiceOntario 4-in-1 Newborn Bundle).
- Note: Do this before you leave the hospital if possible, or within the first week.
