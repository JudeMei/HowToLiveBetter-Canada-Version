# 24. Healthcare: Spend Less, Wait Less

> The Canadian healthcare system is free at the point of use, but navigating it can be slow and confusing. This chapter covers how to get care efficiently.

---

### 1. Know when to use the ER, Urgent Care, or a Walk-in Clinic
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: 
  - **ER (Emergency Room)**: For life- or limb-threatening conditions (chest pain, stroke signs, severe bleeding, difficulty breathing). Wait times for non-emergencies can be 10+ hours because patients are triaged by severity.
  - **Urgent Care**: For issues that need attention today but aren't life-threatening (broken bones, deep cuts needing stitches, severe sprains). Usually faster than the ER.
  - **Walk-in Clinic**: For minor illnesses (strep throat, UTIs, minor rashes) when you can't see your family doctor.
- Payoff: Saves you hours of waiting and keeps the ER clear for true emergencies.
- Evidence: A (Health System Guidelines)
- Note: Call 811 (Health Link / Telehealth) available in most provinces to speak to a registered nurse 24/7 if you aren't sure where to go.

### 2. See your Pharmacist for minor ailments
- Cost: Free (covered by provincial health plans in participating provinces).
- Plain language: In many provinces (including Ontario, BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan, Nova Scotia), pharmacists can now prescribe medications for minor ailments like UTIs, pink eye, cold sores, tick bites (Lyme disease prophylaxis), and uncomplicated skin rashes.
- Payoff: Saves you a trip to a walk-in clinic or doctor's office.
- Evidence: A (Provincial Health Policy)
- Source: Provincial Pharmacy Associations.
- Note: You just walk into the pharmacy and ask. Bring your health card.

### 3. Check if you qualify for the Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP)
- Cost: Subsidized or free dental care.
- Plain language: The federal government is rolling out the CDCP for Canadian residents who do not have access to private dental insurance and have an adjusted family net income under $90,000.
- Payoff: Covers cleanings, fillings, X-rays, and extractions.
- Evidence: A (Federal Policy)
- Source: Health Canada. Canadian Dental Care Plan.
- Note: It is being rolled out in phases (starting with seniors, children under 18, and people with disabilities). You must apply; it is not automatic.

### 4. Apply for the Assistive Devices Program (ADP) if you need medical equipment
- Cost: Government pays 75% or more of the cost.
- Plain language: If you need a wheelchair, hearing aids, CPAP machine, or ostomy supplies, provincial programs (like ADP in Ontario) will cover a massive portion of the cost.
- Payoff: Saves thousands of dollars on necessary medical equipment.
- Evidence: A (Provincial Policy)
- Source: Provincial Ministries of Health.
- Note: You must apply *before* buying the equipment. It usually requires an assessment by a registered authorizer (like an occupational therapist).

### 5. Access virtual care / telehealth
- Cost: Free (if covered by your province).
- Plain language: Many provinces expanded virtual care during the pandemic. If you need a simple prescription renewal or a referral, a virtual appointment is often much faster than waiting to see your family doctor in person.
- Payoff: Saves time and travel.
- Evidence: B
- Note: Check if the service bills your provincial health plan directly. Some private apps charge a fee per visit.

### 6. Do not pay out of pocket for medically necessary services
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: Under the Canada Health Act, it is illegal for clinics to charge you directly for medically necessary services that are covered by your provincial health plan. If a clinic tries to charge you an "access fee" or "membership fee" to see a doctor for a covered service, they are violating the law.
- Payoff: Prevents illegal billing.
- Evidence: A (Canada Health Act)
- Source: Health Canada. Canada Health Act.
- Note: Clinics *can* charge for uninsured services (e.g., doctor's notes for work, transferring medical records, cosmetic procedures).
