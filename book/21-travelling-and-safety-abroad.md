# 21. Travelling & Safety Abroad

> When you leave Canada, you leave behind your provincial health coverage and your Charter rights. This chapter covers how to protect yourself abroad.

---

### 1. Always buy travel medical insurance (Even for a day trip to the US)
- Cost: $20–$100 depending on age and trip length.
- Plain language: Your provincial health plan (e.g., OHIP, MSP) covers almost NOTHING outside of Canada. If you have a heart attack in Florida, or break a leg in a US ski resort, you will be billed tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars.
- Payoff: Prevents medical bankruptcy.
- Evidence: A (Financial reality)
- Source: Government of Canada. Travel insurance.
- Note: Check if your premium credit card or employer health benefits already include travel medical coverage. Read the fine print regarding pre-existing conditions (e.g., if your blood pressure medication was changed in the 90 days before your trip, a heart issue might not be covered).

### 2. Check travel.gc.ca for advisories before booking
- Cost: Free.
- Plain language: The Canadian government issues travel advisories. If a country is under "Avoid non-essential travel" or "Avoid all travel," your travel insurance may be voided, and the Canadian government may not be able to help you if you get in trouble.
- Payoff: Prevents entering war zones, areas with high kidnapping risks, or voiding your insurance.
- Evidence: A (Government Policy)
- Source: Global Affairs Canada. Travel advice and advisories.
- Note: Register with the "Registration of Canadians Abroad" (ROCA) service so the embassy knows where you are if a natural disaster or conflict breaks out.

### 3. Understand the limits of Consular Assistance
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: If you are arrested in a foreign country, the Canadian embassy CANNOT get you out of jail, pay your legal fees, or interfere in local judicial processes. They can only ensure you are treated fairly under local laws, provide a list of local lawyers, and contact your family.
- Payoff: Adjusts expectations. You are subject to the laws of the country you are in, no matter how draconian they seem to a Canadian.
- Evidence: A
- Source: Global Affairs Canada. Consular services.
- Note: Do not carry CBD oil or cannabis across international borders, even if you have a medical prescription and even if it's legal in the destination (like some US states). It is a federal crime at the US border.

### 4. Ensure your passport is valid for at least 6 months beyond your return date
- Cost: $120-$160 for a passport renewal.
- Plain language: Many countries will deny you entry if your passport expires within 3 to 6 months of your arrival date, even if it's valid for the exact dates of your trip.
- Payoff: Prevents being turned away at the airline check-in counter and losing your entire trip cost.
- Evidence: A (International Border Regulations)
- Source: Global Affairs Canada.
- Note: Also ensure your passport is not visibly damaged, water-stained, or torn, as border guards can reject it.

### 5. Get an International Driving Permit (IDP) if driving outside North America
- Cost: $30.
- Plain language: Your Canadian driver's licence is valid in the US, but in many other countries (e.g., Italy, Japan), you are legally required to carry an IDP alongside your Canadian licence. If you are pulled over or in an accident without one, you can be fined and your rental insurance may be voided.
- Payoff: Avoids legal trouble and uninsured liabilities.
- Evidence: A (International Treaties)
- Source: CAA (Canadian Automobile Association — the only authorized issuer in Canada).
- Note: You must get this *before* you leave Canada.

### 6. Use credit cards with No Foreign Transaction Fees
- Cost: Annual fee of $0–$150 for the card.
- Plain language: Most Canadian credit cards charge a hidden 2.5% fee on every purchase made in a foreign currency, on top of the exchange rate. A card with 0% FX fees saves you $25 on every $1,000 you spend abroad.
- Payoff: Significant savings on travel.
- Evidence: B
- Note: Examples include the Scotiabank Passport Visa Infinite, Wealthsimple Cash card (prepaid), or EQ Bank card. When a foreign credit card machine asks if you want to pay in CAD or the local currency, ALWAYS choose the local currency (avoiding Dynamic Currency Conversion).
