# 32. International Students & Studying Abroad

> This chapter covers the legal and financial realities for international students coming to Canada, and Canadians studying abroad.

---

### 1. Ensure your Canadian school is a Designated Learning Institution (DLI)
- Cost: Zero to check.
- Plain language: If you are an international student coming to Canada, you MUST attend a DLI to get a Study Permit. If you want to work in Canada after you graduate, the DLI must be eligible for the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP).
- Payoff: Prevents wasting tens of thousands of dollars on a useless diploma that forces you to leave the country.
- Evidence: A (Immigration Law)
- Source: IRCC. Designated learning institutions list.
- Note: Not all programs at a DLI are eligible for the PGWP. Private career colleges, for example, often are not. Check the specific program requirements carefully.

### 2. Follow the working hours limits strictly
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: International students in Canada can generally work off-campus for a limited number of hours per week during regular school terms (the limit varies based on temporary IRCC policies, historically 20 hours/week, recently adjusted to 24 hours/week).
- Payoff: Avoids deportation.
- Evidence: A (Immigration Law)
- Source: IRCC. Work off campus as an international student.
- Note: If you work even one hour over the legal limit, you violate the conditions of your study permit. This can result in you being ordered to leave Canada and banned from returning.

### 3. Maintain full-time student status
- Cost: Tuition costs.
- Plain language: To keep your study permit and remain eligible for a PGWP, you must actively pursue your studies and maintain full-time status (except for your final semester, or during scheduled breaks like summer).
- Payoff: Protects your immigration status.
- Evidence: A (Immigration Law)
- Source: IRCC.
- Note: Dropping too many classes because they are difficult can ruin your chances of staying in Canada. If you need to take an authorized leave from your studies (e.g., for severe illness), you must clear it with your school and IRCC.

### 4. Buy comprehensive Health Insurance
- Cost: $600–$1,000/year.
- Plain language: Only some provinces (like BC, Alberta, Saskatchewan) provide free provincial health coverage to international students. In others (like Ontario), you MUST buy private health insurance (like UHIP).
- Payoff: Prevents medical bankruptcy if you are hit by a car or get appendicitis.
- Evidence: A (Provincial Policy)
- Note: Usually, the university arranges this automatically as part of your tuition fees, but if you are at a private college, you may need to secure it yourself.

### 5. Canadians studying abroad: Check credential recognition first
- Cost: Zero.
- Plain language: If you are a Canadian citizen going to Ireland to study medicine, or Australia to study law, you must ensure that your degree will be recognized by the Canadian regulatory body when you return.
- Payoff: Prevents spending $200,000 on a degree you cannot use in Canada.
- Evidence: A (Professional Regulation)
- Source: Medical Council of Canada; Federation of Law Societies of Canada (NCA).
- Note: The process of getting a foreign medical degree recognized in Canada (and securing a residency spot) is incredibly difficult and competitive. Many Canadians who study medicine abroad never practice in Canada.

### 6. Beware of immigration fraud and fake agents
- Cost: Saves your life savings.
- Plain language: "Ghost consultants" and fraudulent immigration agents promise guaranteed visas or fake admission letters. If you use a fake document (even if the agent tricked you), IRCC will ban you from Canada for 5 years for misrepresentation.
- Payoff: Avoids a 5-year ban.
- Evidence: A (Immigration Law)
- Source: IRCC. Protect yourself from fraud.
- Note: If you hire an immigration consultant, they MUST be registered with the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC).
